### The Hidden Cost of Depending on Others

Human beings need one another.

Families depend on each other.  
Teams depend on each other.  
Institutions depend on cooperation.  

No one builds anything meaningful completely alone.

So dependence itself is not the problem.

**The problem begins when dependence replaces capability.**

### Asking for Help Is Not Weakness

There is nothing wrong with asking for help.

- A student asking a teacher for guidance is sensible.
- An employee asking an experienced colleague for advice is sensible.
- A person asking for support during a difficult situation is normal.

Independence does not mean refusing help.

**It means being able to move forward when help is not available.**

### When Help Becomes a Habit

The problem appears when a person stops trying before asking.

They do not understand something, so they immediately ask someone else.  
They face a small problem, so they wait for someone to solve it.  
They have a decision to make, so they ask everyone what they should do.  

Slowly, the person may lose confidence in their own judgment.

**The question becomes: *“Who will tell me what to do?”* instead of: *“What can I figure out myself?”***

### Institutions Can Create This Too

Sometimes dependence is not an individual's fault. An institution can create it.

- If students are never trusted with decisions, they may stop taking initiative.
- If employees are punished whenever they make independent decisions, they may wait for instructions.
- If communities expect authorities to solve every local problem, people may stop solving the problems they can handle themselves.

**Dependence can therefore become structural.**

### Capability Comes From Practice

You cannot become independent by reading about independence. You have to practise it:

- Make a decision.
- Take responsibility.
- Make a mistake.
- Correct it.
- Try again.

**That process builds judgment.**

This is why small responsibilities matter. They are practice for larger ones.

### The Cost Is Often Invisible

Dependence may feel comfortable in the short term.

Someone else carries the uncertainty.  
Someone else makes the decision.  
Someone else takes the blame.  

But over time, the dependent person may lose something more valuable:

**Confidence in their own ability.**

And when many people become dependent in the same way, institutions become slower and less flexible.

### The Balance

We should not create a culture where everyone is expected to solve everything alone. That would be another mistake.

Strong societies depend on cooperation. The goal is not complete independence.

**The goal is healthy interdependence:**
- *I can ask you for help without becoming incapable without you.*
- *You can support me without having to carry everything for me.*

That is a healthier relationship.

### The Question

The next time we ask someone to solve a problem for us, perhaps we should pause for a moment:

**“Do I actually need help—or have I simply become uncomfortable trying myself?”**

Sometimes the answer will be that we genuinely need help.  
Sometimes it will reveal a habit.  

**Knowing the difference is itself a form of maturity.**

What feels normal today shapes what becomes unavoidable tomorrow.
