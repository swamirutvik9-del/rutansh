### When Everyone Has a Voice but Nobody Takes Responsibility

We live in a time when almost everyone can speak.

A phone is enough to record a video.  
A few seconds are enough to post an opinion.  
A message can reach hundreds of people instantly.  
A problem can become public within minutes.  

We have more ways to speak than previous generations could have imagined.

But there is a strange question underneath all of this:

**If everyone has a voice, why do so many problems remain someone else's responsibility?**

### Speaking Is Easy

There is an important difference between expressing an opinion and taking responsibility.

- Saying that something is wrong takes seconds. **Fixing it may take months.**
- Complaining about poor education is easy. **Improving one classroom is harder.**
- Talking about cleanliness is easy. **Actually changing behaviour is harder.**
- Demanding accountability is easy. **Accepting accountability ourselves is much harder.**

This does not mean people should remain silent. Speaking up matters.

**But speaking should not become the final step.**

### The Internet Changed Visibility

Earlier, many problems remained invisible because people could not easily show them. Today, visibility is rarely the biggest problem.

A photograph can expose a problem.  
A video can document it.  
A post can bring attention to it.  
People can discuss it.  

And yet, after the discussion ends, the original problem may still be there.

Why?

**Because attention is not responsibility. Someone still has to do the work.**

### The Responsibility Gap

A responsibility gap appears when everyone believes that someone else should act:

- The student expects the teacher.
- The teacher expects the administration.
- The administration expects higher authorities.
- The public expects the government.
- The government may depend on institutions.

And somewhere in the middle, the actual problem remains.

Nobody necessarily intended this outcome.

**It happens because responsibility becomes divided until ownership disappears.**

### We Also Do This Personally

This is not only an institutional problem. We do it in ordinary life:

- A group project fails, and everyone blames the group.
- A friend needs help, and everyone assumes another friend will handle it.
- A mistake happens, and people immediately search for who caused it.

We often ask: *“Who is responsible?”*  
before asking: **“What can I do now?”**

That difference matters.

### Voice Should Lead Somewhere

A healthy society needs people who speak. But it also needs people who act.

The strongest criticism is not always the loudest criticism.

**Sometimes it is the person who quietly takes ownership after everyone else has finished discussing the problem.**

That person may not receive attention. But something actually changes.

### What Should We Expect From Ourselves?

We do not need to personally solve every problem. That is unrealistic.

But whenever we speak about a problem, perhaps we should ask ourselves one additional question:

**“What part of this responsibility belongs to me?”**

Maybe the answer is small. That is still enough to begin.

**Because a society where everyone can speak but nobody takes ownership can become very loud without becoming better.**

What feels normal today shapes what becomes unavoidable tomorrow.
