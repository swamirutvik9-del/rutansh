### Why Young People Need Responsibility, Not Just Opportunities

Whenever we talk about young people, one word appears again and again:

**Opportunity.**

More jobs.  
More scholarships.  
More platforms.  
More internships.  
More technology.  
More chances to build something.  

And yes, young people need opportunities.

**But opportunity alone is not enough.**

### What Happens After Opportunity?

Imagine giving someone a large opportunity without preparing them to handle it.

The opportunity exists. But the ability to use it may not.

This is where responsibility becomes important.

**Responsibility changes the question from: *“What can I get?”* to: *“What can I do with what I have?”***

### Responsibility Creates Capability

A person becomes capable partly by being trusted with real responsibility.

- Managing a small project teaches planning.
- Taking care of a team teaches patience.
- Making a mistake teaches consequences.
- Being responsible for an outcome teaches something that a classroom cannot always provide.

Young people do not become mature simply because they become older.

**They become mature when they begin carrying consequences for their decisions.**

### But Responsibility Without Opportunity Also Fails

There is another side.

Telling young people to “take responsibility” while giving them no meaningful space to act is unfair.

You cannot expect leadership from someone who is never allowed to make decisions.  
You cannot demand experience from someone who is never trusted with work.  
You cannot ask for initiative while punishing every attempt to do something differently.  

**Responsibility and opportunity need each other.**

### The Problem With Waiting

Sometimes young people spend too much time waiting.

Waiting for the perfect opportunity.  
Waiting for someone to notice them.  
Waiting for permission.  
Waiting for a title.  

**But responsibility does not always begin with a title.**

You can take responsibility for a small problem around you:
- You can organise something.
- You can learn a skill.
- You can help someone.
- You can build something that did not exist before.

The scale may be small. **The habit is not.**

### What Society Should Expect

Young people should not only be treated as future citizens.

**They already are citizens.**

They already influence families, institutions, communities, workplaces, and culture.

That means they deserve opportunities. But they also deserve the expectation that their choices matter.

Not because every young person must become extraordinary.

**Simply because every generation eventually inherits the consequences of what it does.**

### The Real Opportunity

Perhaps the greatest opportunity is not being given everything.

**It is being given enough space to try, fail, learn, and take responsibility.**

Because a generation that receives opportunities without responsibility may become dependent on opportunity.

**A generation that receives responsibility along with opportunity can learn how to create it.**

And that difference can shape much more than an individual career.

What feels normal today shapes what becomes unavoidable tomorrow.
