### When Following the Rules Becomes an Excuse

Rules exist for a reason.

They create order. They make expectations clear. They can protect people from unfair treatment and unnecessary confusion.

Without rules, many systems would become difficult to operate.

But there is another side to rules that we rarely discuss:

**Sometimes a person follows the rule perfectly and still produces an unfair result.**

### “I Was Just Following the Rules”

We have all heard some version of this:

- “I can't do anything.”
- “These are the rules.”
- “That's the procedure.”
- “I was only following instructions.”

Sometimes these statements are completely reasonable. A person may genuinely have no authority to change something.

**But sometimes rules become a convenient place to hide from responsibility.**

### The Purpose Can Disappear

Imagine a rule created to prevent misuse.

Over time, people may focus so much on following the procedure that they forget why it existed:

- The paperwork gets completed.
- The boxes are checked.
- The process is followed.

But the actual problem remains. Everything looks correct on paper, yet something is still wrong in reality.

**That is when a system begins serving its procedure rather than its purpose.**

### Rules Cannot Think For Us

A rule can tell us what is normally expected. It cannot always understand every situation.

Real life contains exceptions, unusual circumstances, human consequences, and competing responsibilities.

That is why judgment matters:
- **Following a rule is one form of responsibility.**
- **Understanding its purpose is another.**

Good institutions need both.

### But There Is a Danger Here Too

We should be careful not to use “common sense” as an excuse to ignore every rule we dislike. That creates another problem.

If everyone decides which rules matter based only on personal preference, fairness becomes difficult.

So the answer is not: *“Ignore the rules.”*  
Nor is it: *“Never question the rules.”*

**The better question is: “Does following this rule serve the purpose for which the rule exists?”**

### Questioning Is Not Rebellion

A person who asks why a rule exists is not automatically disrespectful.

Sometimes questioning helps an institution discover a problem in its own process. Rules should be strong enough to create consistency and flexible enough to be reviewed when circumstances change.

**A healthy system should not fear reasonable questions. It should learn from them.**

### Responsibility Does Not End With Procedure

Imagine two people handling the same situation:

- One says: *“I completed the procedure.”*
- The other says: **“I completed the procedure, and I also considered what it means for the person affected.”**

The second person is thinking beyond the paperwork. That difference may seem small. In important situations, it can change everything.

Rules are necessary.

**But rules should remain tools for achieving a purpose—not shields from responsibility.**

What feels normal today shapes what becomes unavoidable tomorrow.
